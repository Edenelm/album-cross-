# Dice album research — RESEARCH PLAN
Working order. One active topic at a time; the next topic starts only when Eden asks.

## Topic 1 — First-album experience (active; decision answered 2026-11-03, see findings top)
**Question.** What does a player experience in their first album, especially when entering in its final third, and what happens in their next album?
**Competing explanations** (none assumed):
- H1 Generous rewards (catch-up, free packs and rolls) delay the need to buy.
- H2 Too little progress (duplicates, slow new stickers) discourages play.
- H3 Generous early rewards build engagement and future value.
- H4 General new-player drop-off that the album only reflects.
- H5 Measurement artefacts: history floor, entry definition, unequal exposure, incomplete follow-up.
Detailed plan: `topics/t1-first-album/PLAN.md`.

## Topic 2 — Path to completion (not started)
**Question.** Players missing exactly the three locked stickers, 4+ days before close: how do they move 3 → 2 → 1 → 144, how long, what they spend, which sources fill each sticker, and where they stop?
**Comparison.** Finishers vs non-finishers who started in similar circumstances: spend before the comparison point, days left, roll balance, progress. Not matched on total album spend (part of it is the completion journey itself).
**Inputs:** `topics/t2-completion-path/README.md`.

## Topic 3 — Trail's real difficulty (not started)
**Question.** At album end the Trail is set harder and awards a Wild that can fill a locked sticker. Is it hard in practice, and does a commercially meaningful group finish it fast through a chain of actions and rewards from other features?
**Trace.** Starting state → purchases and actions → feature completions and rewards → Trail progress → Wild → album progress. Compare non-payers and players with similar spend: starting balances, progress in other features, offers available, activity, luck. No claims about intent.
**Inputs:** `topics/t3-trail-difficulty/README.md`.

## Rules for every topic
Before: business question and explanations, population, unit, window, denominator, evidence for each explanation. Checks: duplicates, join inflation, missing values, dates, event order, economic sense, reconciliation with a known number. Biases: exposure time, self-selection, survivorship, population mixing, future-information leakage, correlation vs causation. Each finding: what we saw (N, % of players, revenue share), business meaning, what is supported vs uncertain, recommendation or next check. No deck until the story holds.
