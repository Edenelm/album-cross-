# Album deck — story order (2026-10-20)

One population at a time. Each chapter ends with what that studio does next. A recommendation sits next to the fact that can prove it wrong. Charts carry the slide. Text stays short.

Payers, not the first album, login ratio at least 0.5. A finisher has 144 unique stickers by the end of the close day. The close day is left out only from end-of-album ARPDAU, because it is a half day. Revenue share is album spend in that population. Targets of 30% revenue share and about 10% of payers finishing are Eden's targets, not a measured optimum. Dice albums 61 and 63, Domino 17, 19 and 20, Disney 14 and 15.

Dice opens the deck because the prize and the slow return are Dice moves. That can make Dice look like the model. Domino album 20 and the Disney near-finish group are in the deck to break that. Where a sentence is a guess, it is marked Hypothesis, and the next line contradicts it.

Show both albums, then the average. An average of the two Dice albums hides that album 63 is close to the revenue target and album 61 is not.

## 1. Dice — the people who pay to finish

Population: payers who reach 144. Revenue share: 23% and 28% of album spend. Their album ARPDAU is about $8, and about $17 on the last four full days. Completion day is about $43, and 91% of that day is before the finish moment.

Candidate: a larger final prize for the reward tier that already spends a lot of rolls and pays to finish. In Dice, that tier is album reward code 1–7, not the difficulty segment.

Hypothesis: a bigger prize pulls more of this strong group over the line, and their revenue share moves toward 30%.

Contradiction: these players already pay to finish. A larger prize can pay people who would finish anyway. The reward ladder only moves up, and this cut by reward code is not measured yet. The players who are strong and do not finish are a different group: in album 63, 48% of payers stop on 10 of 12 Supers and hold 40% of album spend, and their last four days are 0.55 of their album ARPDAU. A prize aimed at finishers misses that 40%.

Next measurement, before the prize is designed: finisher revenue share by reward code 1–7. If they already sit on codes 6 and 7, the prize is a raise for the top, not a new path.

## 2. The day after — payout, then play

Three groups, all three games: finished, one sticker left, and 2–3 stickers left. The chart is payout with purchases taken out, on the day after the album. Revenue share of each group sits on the same slide. This cut is not in the saved results yet.

Hypothesis: finishers receive a larger payout the next day, and that payout is what brings them back.

Contradiction: a larger payout can be the prize for finishing, not the reason they play. The groups that did not finish may receive less and still show up, or receive the same and leave. Activity the week after is already about 91–94% for finishers and for players who were 2–3 short. Payout without purchases has to be read next to that, or a rolls chart will be mistaken for a return chart.

## 3. Dice — Lava Quest and Fortune Wheel

The content already exists: Fortune Wheel plus the finisher quest, which is the Lava Quest. The question is whether players reach it. Chart: share of finishers who enter it, share of everyone else who enters it, and whether the people who enter keep playing. Revenue share of each of those groups on the same slide.

Hypothesis: finishers who reach both keep playing. Players who never reach them are the ones who slow down.

Contradiction: reaching the content and choosing to play are the same kind of player. A higher play rate among people who entered does not show that the quest caused it. The quest is built for finishers, so a low reach among everyone else can be the design. Earlier Lava tests are a different question and do not answer reach in these albums.

## 4. Dice — the days after they finish

Same people, next window. Days 1–7 versus their own base: Dice +9% and −1%. Domino +8% and +16%. Disney +10% and +16%. The gap is sharper for players who finished the day before close, in the first three days of the next album: Dice 0.88 and 0.74, Domino 0.90 and 0.96, Disney 0.95 and 1.00. Day 1 of the next album is about 60% of the Dice base.

Candidate: do not add another offer on the completion day. Test a pack that opens with the next album, on Dice players who finished the day before close. Do not design that pack until chapters 2 and 3 are measured.

Hypothesis: Dice finishers come back slower than Disney and Domino, so an opening pack makes them spend again sooner.

Contradiction: album 61 is +9% over days 1–7. The slow album is 63, at −1%. An average would hide that. Domino album 17 is close to Dice on those first three days (0.90 versus 0.88). Players at $20 a day are flat to −9% after finish in all three games, so the strong group is not a Dice-only problem. Draining them on the completion day fights the spike: 91% of that day's spend already happened before they finished.

## 5. Domino — album 20 finished more

Album 19 to album 20: finish rate 4.5% to 8.0%, finisher revenue share 23% to 31%, spend per payer $114 to $120, total album spend $13.6 million to $12.8 million, spend per finisher $573 to $461. The royal that closed the album came from Puzzle for 31% of finishers and from Movie Twister for 25%. A joker closed it for 2%. Duplicate exchange closed it for 2 finishers.

Candidate, Eden's read: the Puzzle joker is the reward of a hard event, usually finished with a purchase, and album 20 gave two. Test one Puzzle joker against two.

Hypothesis: two jokers made the album easier to finish and cheaper per finisher. One joker keeps the finish rate and brings spend per finisher back up.

Contradiction: the last-royal chart does not show that the second joker caused the change. Movie Twister closed the album for a quarter of finishers. Total spend fell because there were fewer payers, not only because finishers spent less. Album 20 is the one album on the 30% line. Cutting a joker can give that line back. Earlier Domino albums already showed that more gameplay jokers did not raise the finish rate of players who were 2–3 short.

## 6. Disney — royals, then the joker

Five days before close, players missing 0–3 stickers are 13% and 10% of payers and about 40% of album spend. Their last four days are 1.03 and 1.05 of their album ARPDAU. In Dice and Domino that ratio is 1.16 to 1.26. In the last seven days, payers who used a joker from a purchase, the market, or another source spent 1.28 to 1.50 times the prior week. Payers with no joker are 90–93% of payers, and their spend fell to 0.64 and 0.77. On market-joker days, 60% and 46% of that route's week fell on the collection day.

Two candidates, two tests: more royals, and a joker offer that starts about seven days before close, for players already close. Not in the same test.

Hypothesis: Disney finishes less because it has 7 royals and sells the joker late. More royals, and a joker a week out, raise finish rate and the spend of the near group.

Contradiction: more royal slots can build the Dice wall. Album 63 parks 40% of spend on players stuck at 10 of 12 Supers. More supply of the existing 7 royals is a different change. Days 10–8 already show a thinner royal supply, and the effect on spend was not measured. The joker routes chose themselves. Selling seven days earlier can move the last-day purchase forward. In Domino, a shorter joker sprint did not raise the finish rate of players who were 2–3 short. Seven days is a proposal, not a measured best day. At the same distance and the same spend, Disney already spends at a similar ratio and finishes less, so the open question is completion, not only willingness to pay.

The near group is about 40% of album spend. The $20-a-day payers missing 2–3 at the end are 8% and 9%. Those are not the same people seven days earlier. That cut is not measured yet.
